# Where Grove Chess could go

Options, not decisions. Written 2026-10-06 against Timothy's three-part
shape in [`../INTENT.md`](../INTENT.md): a shareable daily puzzle, an
open-ended world to explore, and other players woven in. The point of this
page is to narrow, so each section ends with a recommendation.

Every option here keeps the house rules: nothing decays, nothing nags, no
streak to break, and no server unless we decide one is worth it.

## 1. The daily: one shareable puzzle

What exists already works as the core: one board a day from the date, par
from a solver, your result is a link, links grow a vine.

What the lab can now tell us before changing it: which goal makes the best
daily (catch the rabbit, take the King, sink the ball), how big a board, and
what par range feels good. Those answers come from the lab notebook, not from
guessing.

**Recommendation:** keep the daily small and legible. One goal per day, and
perhaps let the goal rotate through the week (Monday rabbit, Tuesday ball,
and so on) once the lab shows each one can be fun on its own.

## 2. The branch: playing someone's upgraded version

The idea: when you play from a friend's link, you are not playing the same
puzzle they did. You are playing their version of it, changed a little by
them, and whoever plays from yours gets your version. The vine becomes a line
of related puzzles that a friend group builds together.

Three concrete ways it could work. All of them fit in the link, so still no
server.

**A. Gifts.** When you finish, you add one thing to the board for whoever
plays from your link: a stump, an extra piece for them, a second rabbit, a
nudge to the hole. The next player gets the day's board plus every gift up
their branch. The solver re-checks par for each version, which is fast for
daily-sized boards. Gifts can be kind or mischievous, and choosing one is a
small, specific thing you do *for* a particular friend.

**B. Inheritance.** Your result changes the next board automatically. Catch
it fast and the rabbit gets a little smarter for the next person; get caught
out and they start with an extra piece. Self-balancing, and no decisions to
make, but less personal.

**C. Relay.** One shared game instead of many. Each person on the branch
plays a few moves of the same board and passes the position on. The group
finishes the puzzle together, and the score is the group's.

**Recommendation:** start with A. It is the smallest to build, it is the one
where you are doing something for a specific friend (which is the point of
this repo), and it tests whether "your version of the puzzle" is interesting
before anything bigger depends on it.

## 3. The pot: what the group builds over time

The long-term progression the earlier intent asked for, at group scale.
Every catch on your vine plants something in a garden that belongs to the
chat, and everyone on the vine sees the same garden, because every browser
already keeps the union of every game it has been shown. It only grows. A
chat that has played for a month has a garden that looks like a month.

This is also where the original PET framing (an abandoned forest coming back
to life) can return without the two-person constraint: the group's play
reawakens the place.

**Recommendation:** build it right after gifts, as a picture on the end
screen first. It costs little and it is the answer to "why come back".

## 4. The world: open-ended exploration

*Worked out further in [`grove-long-game.md`](grove-long-game.md): a
single-player mode that holds the long-term progression, with the history
of chess as its spine.*

A map of clearings, each a level built on the lab engine and checked by the
solver, each teaching or stretching one idea: a rabbit warren, a King's
court, a putting green where the ball and hole live, a bramble maze.
Clearings open as you finish others. It is where the lab's findings go to
live once they are proven fun.

Collaborative variants later: friends opening clearings for each other, or a
shared map the group explores together.

**Recommendation:** last of the four. It is the biggest, and it should be
built from things the notebook says are fun rather than designed ahead of
them.

## Suggested order

1. Play the lab and rate levels until three or four kinds are clearly fun.
2. Branch gifts on the daily (option 2A).
3. The pot garden on the end screen.
4. The first handful of world clearings, from the notebook's favourites.
