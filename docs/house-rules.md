# House rules

Rules that apply to everything in this collection. If a game contradicts one of
these, the game is wrong.

## 1. Both people play

The failure mode of every conversation game is that it becomes an interview.
One person asks, the other performs, and forty minutes later one of you is
tired and the other one feels like they were on a podcast.

Every prompt gets answered by both people. If you named the prompt, you answer
second — you've had longer to think, so you owe the harder answer.

## 2. Anyone can stop, and stopping isn't a failure

"I don't want to play this tonight" is a complete sentence. So is "can we do
the easy one instead." A game you were talked into is worse than no game.

Agree in advance that either of you can call it, and that calling it never
requires a reason.

## 3. Don't fix, unless asked

Games like [Rose, Bud, Thorn](../games/rose-bud-thorn.md) and
[The First Time I…](../games/the-first-time-i.md) will surface something hard.
The reflex is to solve it. Ask first:

> "Do you want help with that, or do you want me to just be annoyed about it
> with you?"

Getting this wrong is the single most common way a good call turns into a bad
one.

## 4. The competitive games stay small

[Ghost](../games/ghost.md) and [Category Ping-Pong](../games/category-ping-pong.md)
are genuinely competitive, and that's good — not every night has to be
meaningful. But keep the stakes to the round. Nobody should be defending a
championship. If one of you is consistently better, use the rule that gives the
loser the advantage (loser picks the category, loser goes first).

## 5. Match the game to the energy in the room

The most common mistake isn't picking a bad game, it's picking a good game at
the wrong time. Someone who got on the phone exhausted cannot play
[The 36 Questions](../games/thirty-six-questions.md), and pushing them through
it will teach them that these calls are work.

There's a whole page on this: [choosing-a-game.md](choosing-a-game.md).

## 6. End before it runs out

Stop while it's still good. Every one of these games has a natural point where
you've had the fun and you're now doing rounds out of momentum. Stop one round
earlier than that.

## 7. It's not really about the game

The game is a pretext for spending unstructured time together, which is the
thing that's actually hard to arrange from two hundred miles apart. If you
abandon the rules twenty minutes in because a real conversation broke out —
that was a successful game.
