Custom

Any pieces, any board, any rules. Play it, let the bots simulate it, or have them find the most playable way to stand the armies. Rate what you play, and it goes in the notebook. A level is its settings plus a dice roll, so its link always rebuilds it exactly.

This game in eight parts

How this level plays

    Armies

    Dealt: each side is dealt from the pools in the settings below. Square by square: you choose your army, piece by piece and in order, with suits; theirs is the same, mirrored or turned. Tap a piece in your line to give it a suit, move it, or take it out.

    Simulate, and find the most playable layout

    Simulate: two equal bots play this level many times, and a stronger one plays a weaker one, on the real rules, and the games are judged. Find: every way to stand the armies (or, dealt, many dice rolls) is checked, the best are improved a swap at a time, and the best few judged.

    How the bots judge a game

    The bots play on the real rules: every setting, suit, ghost, gate and cloud. Each looks two moves ahead (a minimax search with alpha-beta pruning, the oldest idea in computer chess), then keeps following captures until the position is quiet, so it does not stop counting halfway through a trade. Under clouds each plans from what it can see.

    The measures follow Cameron Browne’s Ludi (2008), a program that invented board games and judged them by self-play: balance, how often a game is decided, length, drama, uncertainty, whether skill pays, whether every piece plays, and variety. With a handful of games each, scores within about ten of each other are too close to call. They are a bot’s guesses. Play the ones that look good: your opinion outranks theirs.

    Weave in a piece

    A new piece, or one you invented in the pieces, with a suit if you like: swapped in for each kind of piece in your army, one at a time (and added, if there is room), each army searched for its best layout.

    Notebook

    Every level you rated, newest first. Load one to play it again.

    Autochess

    Put a rabbit in each piece. The rabbit picks the way, the piece picks how, every turn, by itself. Pick a place in your line, then a rabbit. A piece with no rabbit stands still.
