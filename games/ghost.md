# Ghost

> You spell a word one letter at a time, and you lose if you finish it. All
> the tension of Scrabble, none of the board.

**Works over:** voice
**Time:** 5–15 min
**Energy:** medium — you have to actually think
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. Player A says a letter. Say `C`.
2. Player B adds a letter to the end. `C-A`.
3. Keep alternating. Every letter added must keep a real word *possible* — you
   are always spelling toward some word you have in mind.
4. You **lose the round** if you complete a word of four letters or more.
   `C-A-R-D` — that's a word, that's a loss.
5. You also lose if you're **challenged and caught**. If your opponent thinks
   no word can follow your letter, they say "challenge." You have to name the
   word you were building. If you can, they lose the round. If you can't, you
   lose.
6. Each round lost is a letter of `G-H-O-S-T`. Spell the whole word, you're
   out.

## The rules that matter

- **Words of three letters or fewer are free.** Otherwise the game ends in two
  moves every time.
- **Challenging is the good part.** New players never challenge and the game
  stalls into thirty-letter nonsense strings. Challenge early, challenge often
  — the bluffs are where the laughing happens.
- **Say the whole string every time.** "C-A-R… car." Over a phone you will
  both lose track by letter six otherwise.
- **Agree on a dictionary before you start.** Not really — but agree on who
  gets the benefit of the doubt, which in practice is whoever is less
  competitive that night.

## Variations

- **Superghost** — you can add letters to *either end* of the string. `A-R` can
  become `C-A-R` or `A-R-M`. Vastly harder, and the best version if you've
  played a lot.
- **Category Ghost** — every word must be a food, or an animal, or a place.
  Narrows the space enough that a challenge is genuinely risky.

## Why it works

Ghost is the most competitive game in this collection, which is why it's here
— not every night has to be tender. It's also the one you can play at a full
walking pace with your phone in your pocket, which makes it the default for
"I'm walking home, stay on the line."
