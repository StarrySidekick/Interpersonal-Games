# Rose, Bud, Thorn

> Three things about your day: the good one, the coming one, the hard one. It
> takes four minutes and it replaces "how was your day" forever.

**Works over:** voice, video, text
**Time:** 5 min
**Energy:** any
**Players:** 2

## How to play

Each of you gives three things about your day:

1. **Rose** — something good that happened. Small counts. *The coffee was
   right. Someone held a door.*
2. **Bud** — something you're looking forward to. Also small. *Tomorrow is
   Friday. There's a new episode.*
3. **Thorn** — something that was hard, annoying, or sad.

One person does all three, then the other. Then you talk about whichever one
wants talking about.

## The rules that matter

- **Thorn last.** People are more honest about the hard thing once they've
  already said two easy things out loud. Reordering this breaks it.
- **Don't fix the thorn.** The listener's job is "that sounds bad," not "have
  you tried." Ask before you problem-solve: *"Do you want help with that or do
  you want me to just be annoyed with you?"*
- **Small is allowed.** The whole value is that it works on ordinary days. If
  the rose has to be impressive, you'll skip it on the days you most need it.

## Variations

- **Bud only** — for a morning call.
- **Rose for the other person** — name something good *they* did or told you
  about. Harder, and lands differently.
- **Written, daily** — three lines in a text, sent whenever you wake up. This
  is the version that survives a time difference.

## Why it works

"How was your day" is a question with a default answer, and the default answer
is "fine." Three named slots make "fine" impossible without being an
interrogation. It's a format, not a game — but it's the thing to start with if
you talk every day and the calls have gotten thin.

Widely used in education and outdoor programs; origin is folk, no single
author.
