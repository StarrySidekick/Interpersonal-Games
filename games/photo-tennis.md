# Photo Tennis

> One photo each, back and forth, no rush. A game designed for two people who
> are almost never awake at the same time.

**Works over:** text, async
**Time:** ongoing — one turn a day, or one a week
**Energy:** almost none
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. One of you sends a photo taken **today**. No caption.
2. The other replies with a photo of their own that **answers** it somehow —
   same color, same shape, same subject, same joke, opposite of it. Their call.
3. Repeat, indefinitely. There is no score and no end.
4. Once a week or so, one of you sends a photo and *does* caption it — the
   story behind it. Then back to silence.

## The rules that matter

- **Taken today.** Not from the camera roll. This is the rule that makes the
  game mean something: it's proof of a day, not a curated image.
- **No captions, mostly.** The connection between the two photos should have to
  be figured out. Explaining it deflates it.
- **No obligation to be quick.** A turn can take three days. The game surviving
  a slow patch is a feature, not a failure — the thread is still open.
- **Bad photos are correct.** A blurry photo of a parking lot is a better turn
  than a good photo of a sunset.

## Variations

- **Themed weeks** — agree on a constraint on Monday. *Doorways. Things that
  are red. Other people's dogs.*
- **Sound tennis** — voice notes instead. Ten seconds of whatever is audible
  where you are. Weirdly intimate; try it once.
- **The annual print** — at the end of a year, one of you prints the whole
  exchange in order. This is the real reason to play.

## Why it works

Distance is mostly a scheduling problem: the calls you manage are a small
fraction of the days you both live. This puts a low, steady signal in between
— evidence that the other person had a Tuesday. It asks for about fifteen
seconds a day, which is the only budget that survives an actual busy life.
