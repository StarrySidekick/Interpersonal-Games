# Would You Rather, And Why

> Standard Would You Rather with one rule added: you have to defend it. The
> "and why" is what turns a party game into a conversation.

**Works over:** voice, text
**Time:** 5–20 min
**Energy:** low
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. One of you poses a dilemma: two options, both plausible, neither obviously
   better.
2. The other answers — and gives **at least one real sentence of why**. An
   answer without a why doesn't count.
3. The asker then has to answer their own question. No posing a dilemma you
   haven't thought about.
4. Swap.

## The rules that matter

- **Both people answer every question.** Including the asker. This is the whole
  rule set, really.
- **"Both" and "neither" are illegal.** Pick one. Dodging is the failure mode
  of this game.
- **Make the options close.** A good Would You Rather is one where you change
  your mind halfway through explaining yourself. If your partner answers
  instantly, the question was bad — write a better one.

## Starter dilemmas

Would you rather… always be 20 minutes early or 10 minutes late · know when
you'll die or how · lose every photo you've taken or every message you've sent
· be able to speak every language or play every instrument · live somewhere
beautiful and far from everyone, or somewhere ordinary near everyone · never
be able to leave your country again or never be able to return to it · have
your work be loved but not by you, or loved by you and no one else.

## Variations

- **About us** — dilemmas involving the two of you specifically. Handle with
  care; this can go from funny to a real conversation in one question, which is
  sometimes exactly what you want.
- **Guess first** — before answering, guess what *they'll* pick. Getting it
  wrong is the interesting outcome.
- **Escalate** — each round, the asker must make the dilemma harder than the
  last one. Ends somewhere absurd in about six rounds.

## Why it works

The "and why" is doing all the work. Anyone can pick an option; explaining it
means saying something about what you value, and doing that inside a silly
frame is far easier than doing it directly. The guess-first variation is the
one that will actually surprise you about someone you've known for years.
