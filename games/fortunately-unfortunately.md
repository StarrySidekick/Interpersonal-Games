# Fortunately, Unfortunately

> You build a story one sentence at a time, and every sentence has to reverse
> the last one. It goes badly. That's the game.

**Works over:** voice, text
**Time:** 5–20 min
**Energy:** low
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. Player A opens with a plain sentence. *"A man missed his train."*
2. Player B answers, starting with **"Fortunately…"** — something good happens.
   *"Fortunately, the next one came in four minutes."*
3. Player A answers, starting with **"Unfortunately…"** — something bad
   happens. *"Unfortunately, it was going the wrong way."*
4. Keep alternating fortunately / unfortunately until one of you can't stop
   laughing or the story reaches something that feels like an ending.

## The rules that matter

- **One sentence.** Not a paragraph. The rhythm is the whole game, and long
  turns kill it.
- **Accept what came before.** You can't undo your partner's sentence — no
  "actually he wasn't at a station at all." You can only add. This is the
  improv rule and it's non-negotiable.
- **Escalate.** If your fortunately is as small as their unfortunately, the
  story flatlines. Each turn should be a bigger swing than the last.

## Variations

- **Three-word turns** — brutal, fast, very funny.
- **Real life edition** — the story starts with something that actually
  happened to one of you today. Turns a bad day into a bit, which is a
  genuinely useful thing to be able to do at 11pm.
- **Written, async** — one sentence each, whenever. A story that takes a month
  and gets absurd in slow motion. See also [Photo Tennis](photo-tennis.md).

## Why it works

Two people making one thing is the fastest way to feel like a unit. And the
forced reversal means neither of you can steer — you end up somewhere neither
of you would have gone alone, which is the difference between a story and a
conversation.
