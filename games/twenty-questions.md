# Twenty Questions

> One of you thinks of a thing. The other has twenty yes-or-no questions to
> find it. It is the oldest remote game there is, and it still works.

**Works over:** voice, text
**Time:** 5–10 min per round
**Energy:** low — playable while driving, cooking, or lying in the dark
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. One player (the **holder**) thinks of something and says which category it
   falls in: animal, vegetable, mineral, or abstract.
2. The other player (the **guesser**) asks questions that can be answered
   *yes*, *no*, or *sort of*.
3. The guesser gets twenty questions. A guess at the actual answer costs a
   question.
4. If the guesser gets it, they win. If they burn all twenty, the holder wins
   — and has to reveal the answer.
5. Swap roles.

## The rules that matter

- **"Sort of" is a legal answer.** Without it the holder ends up lying by
  technicality and the game turns sour. Use it honestly.
- **The holder can't change their mind.** Pick the thing, commit to it. If you
  realize your answer was ambiguous, say so and give a free question.
- **Say the question number out loud.** "That's eleven." It's the whole tension
  of the game, and over a phone call it's easy to lose count.

## Variations

- **Fifteen Questions** — for a short call. Shrinks the game to about four
  minutes and forces better questions.
- **Personal edition** — the answer must be something from your shared life: a
  place you've both been, a person you both know, a thing that happened. This
  is the version that makes the game about the two of you rather than about
  trivia.
- **Reverse** — the guesser can only ask questions the holder has never been
  asked before. Slower, funnier, much harder.

## Why it works

Twenty Questions is the rare game where the fun is in the *other person's*
thinking. You spend ten minutes watching how someone narrows a space, what
they consider obvious, what they'd never think to ask. Played with the
personal edition, it doubles as a memory tour.
