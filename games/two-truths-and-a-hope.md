# Two Truths and a Hope

> Two Truths and a Lie, with the lie replaced by something you actually want.
> The guessing is the same. What you learn is much better.

**Works over:** voice, video
**Time:** 10 min
**Energy:** medium
**Players:** 2

## How to play

1. One player says three statements about themselves: **two things that are
   true**, and **one thing that isn't true yet, but that they'd like to be.**
2. The other player guesses which is the hope.
3. Right or wrong — you then talk about the hope. That's the actual game; the
   guess is just the way in.
4. Swap.

## The rules that matter

- **The hope has to be real.** Not a joke hope, not "I'd like to own a
  helicopter." Something you'd actually do something about. This is the rule
  the whole game rests on.
- **The two truths should be surprising too.** If they're facts your partner
  already knows, the hope is obvious and there's nothing to guess.
- **The talking-about-it is mandatory.** Guess, reveal, then ask one real
  question about the hope. Don't move straight to the next round.

## Variations

- **Two Truths and a Fear** — same shape, harder, only for people who already
  trust each other.
- **About each other** — say three things about *your partner*, two true and
  one you hope for them. Unexpectedly moving. Don't do this one first.
- **Classic** — two truths and a plain lie, if the night calls for something
  lighter.

## Why it works

The standard version teaches you trivia about someone. This version teaches
you what they want, which is the thing you actually run out of ways to ask
about once you've known someone a while. The guessing format gives them
deniability if the answer is embarrassing, which is why it gets a truer answer
than "so what do you want out of the next year."
